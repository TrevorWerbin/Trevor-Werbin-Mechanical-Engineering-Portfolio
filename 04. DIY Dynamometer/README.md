**Skills:** Excel, Data Analysis, Technical Report Writing

**Objective:** Design and build a dynamometer to measure torque output of a TT motor using materials from a TETRIX® Programmable Robotics Set and a switch, breadboard, jumper wires and power source to operate the motor for a ME100 project. We were also given other materials to optionally use including nuts and bolts (for weights), elastic bands, string and a plastic cup. The project required us to create either a rope or Prony brake dynamometer only using information we found online.

**Outcome:** I collaborated with four other mechanical engineers to create a rope brake dynamometer since it required less material to build than the Prony brake. We submitted a report evaluating the accuracy of our torque measurements compared to the manufacturer’s values and identifying potential sources of inaccuracy.

**Responsibilities:** I designed the final structure which featured a tall frame that allowed for a wider range of measurements compared to designing the dynamometer to hang off of a table, which was the alternative. I compared the displacement of the elastic band to the elastic force and the torque to the speed of the TT motor in two scatter plots that I created with best fit lines in Excel. I also authored the section of the report about the torque-speed curve and sources of measurement inaccuracy.

**Learning:** I gained an understanding of the purpose and function of dynamometers, including the difference between rope and Prony brake types, and strengthened my data analysis skills by analyzing correlations in the graphs. I also improved my skills in translating research (about rope brakes) into a physical working product.
